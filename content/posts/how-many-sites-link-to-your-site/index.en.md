<span style="color: #ffffff;">I'm sure you're all curious about which websites contain links to your site! Here I'll show you how to search with the link syntax. It's a Google operator that lists the websites that link to your site, </span><span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">and note that the unit here is websites, not pages, </span><span style="color: #ffffff;">so let Ting-feng walk you through this syntax!</span></span><span style="color: #ffffff;"> </span>

<span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;"> </span></span>
<pre lang="html"><span style="color: #ffffff;">Syntax:
link:URL

Replace URL with your site's domain, without "http://"</span></pre>

<!--more-->
<span style="color: #ffffff;">After seeing the syntax above, you're probably thinking:</span><span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">Okay, the syntax is simple, but where do I paste it to run it?</span></span>

<span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">Haha, it's even simpler than that.</span></span>

<span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">First, open the Google search engine, then type the syntax into the search box and search.</span></span>

</span> </span></span>

<span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">Then you'll see Google list the websites that link to your site.</span></span>

<span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #ffffff;">You can also check the "results" count in the upper right corner.
