Recently I needed a new phone. This is actually my first mobile phone. To pay for it, I even sold the iPod Touch I bought last year. I had bought the iPod Touch because the school had a rule against phones. But now that I've moved on to vocational high school, the school doesn't really care about phones, and it even has WiFi. My mom also set up a phone plan for me, so I saved up and bought this little IDEOS phone～ To be honest, the story is pretty funny... Buying an HTC Hero would have meant waiting until December, when I'd have NT$4,000 in income. But since I had sold the iPod Touch, I had nothing to get online with, and that was really hard to put up with...... I'm used to pulling the iPod Touch out of my pocket to go online whenever I want, so I couldn't stand it and rushed to the phone shop to buy the cheapest IDEOS. I'll save next month's NT$4,000 instead～


<!--more-->


At first I spent a long time deciding at the phone shop, because the IDEOS's specs really look bad.... Its price is the lowest in Taiwan, but the specs are cut down quite a bit (thankfully, not the worst specs in all of Taiwan XD). Its hardware is about the same level as the HTC Tattoo. The difference is that its screen is capacitive, while the Tattoo's is resistive, which responds more slowly. But the Tattoo runs Android 1.6, while this IDEOS runs 2.2. From my experience with the iPod Touch, I knew higher version numbers meant more performance drain, so when I bought it, I was really worried its hardware was too weak. But after actually using it, I found my worry was unnecessary. Android seems to run better with higher version numbers. At 528MHz, there's not a hint of lag. For the price, it's a great deal.

The IDEOS's main advantage is that it runs Android 2.2, so it supports many new features, such as moving apps to the SD card, tethering (sharing the network), a PIN lock screen, and Flash 10.1. Its capacitive touchscreen also makes touch response very fast. The back cover can be swapped, and the manufacturer includes three colored covers. I haven't seen any aftermarket accessories for it on the market yet. As for RAM, 256MB is enough. Now for the downsides. First, it doesn't support multi-touch, which really annoys me. They gave us a capacitive screen, so why no multi-touch... Also, the camera has no autofocus, and it's only 3.2 megapixels, so the photos are okay, I guess. The last downside is that the screen is small, only 2.8 inches, so the virtual keyboard is a bit hard to hit. I recommend using handwriting input. Android's handwriting input surprised me: its accuracy is close to 100%, and it works for both Chinese and English, unlike iOS, which only handles Chinese. Also, because the screen and resolution are small, some games won't run at all, so if you want to play games, this phone isn't for you. A netizen also mentioned that if the battery dies later, it'll be a hassle. Unless you send it back to the manufacturer, replacement batteries are hard to find, so buy one now while it's still new on the market, and get a spare battery with next month's NT$4,000~ I recommend the full package. The basic package only comes with one battery.
