As everyone knows, Apple is famously ruthless with its rivals, and the latest news makes that clear. For example, none of Apple's products support Adobe's Flash. The reason given is that Flash eats up resources and has lots of security holes. But some people tested other smartphones with the same specs as the iPhone and found they didn't use as many resources as Apple claimed. Meanwhile, Adobe added a Package for iPhone to its new Creative Suite 5, which lets users convert Flash files into a format that works on the iPhone / iPod Touch. <span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong>Apple immediately changed the rules in the iPhone Developer Program License Agreement to ban the use of the Package for iPhone technology</strong></span>. It's a clear sign that Apple doesn't welcome Flash. But what does this have to do with Opera Mini?


<!--more-->


On the night of April 13, at around 11:30, I suddenly got a message from a reader named Jeremy saying that <a href="http://www.opera.com/mobile/">Opera Mini</a> had been approved. I thought, no way! Given Apple's approach to its rivals, how could it let Opera take over the turf of its own <a href="http://www.apple.com/tw/safari/">Safari</a>? Had Apple really given in to <a href="http://www.nownews.com/2010/03/24/339-2583469.htm">Opera's video offensive</a>? It's hard to believe Apple would let Opera through, but the fact is that <a href="http://www.opera.com/mobile/">Opera Mini</a> really did make it onto the App Store. I don't understand why, but since Apple has already let Opera Mini in, it seems a shame not to give it a try~

Opera Mini official website: <a href="http://www.opera.com/mobile/">http://www.opera.com/mobile/</a>
<h2>Advantages of Opera Mini over <a href="http://www.apple.com/tw/safari/">Safari</a>:</h2>
<ol>
	<li>Faster web page loading</li>
	<li>Built-in mobile browsing mode</li>
	<li>Built-in option to turn images on or off</li>
	<li>Speed Dial, similar to Google's</li>
</ol>

2.Tap FREE → Install in the upper right corner.

3.Sign in with your iTunes account.

9.【Settings inside the wrench button】 let you adjust font size, image quality, whether images load, and whether to turn on mobile display mode. Mobile display mode strips out unnecessary style sheets so you can read a web page without scrolling side to side.

<h2>Impressions:</h2>
In my testing, <a href="http://www.opera.com/mobile/">Opera Mini</a> is definitely much faster than Safari, but it still has some drawbacks. For example, Opera Mini's JavaScript support isn't good enough. It's not just Flash that won't display: sometimes JavaScript won't run at all. When I browsed this blog with Opera, for instance, the image enlargement and LazyLoad features didn't work. Also, since Opera Mini is essentially a separate app, it's not easy to import bookmarks. For people who already have lots of bookmarks saved in Safari, moving them over is a real hassle. Opera Mini also can't use Safari's "Add to Home Screen" feature. Still, as much as I complain, Opera does beat Safari by a good margin when it comes to speed, and the Speed Dial feature is very handy.
