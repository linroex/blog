Yesterday, while reading the newspaper, I came across an article saying that a supervisor at 104 Job Bank had <a href="http://tw.news.yahoo.com/article/url/d/a/100119/11/1z1hm.html">been monitoring employees' instant messages</a>. I won't comment on that, since it's an adult matter. From the company's point of view, of course they want to take measures to prevent employees from leaking information, and the company had told employees that instant messaging software would be monitored. But from the employee's point of view, it must be really annoying, since nobody likes being watched. Oops, I've drifted off topic a bit~~ Anyway, this post is going to teach you to use two programs: <strong>MSNShell</strong> and <strong>SimpLite-Yahoo.</strong>

<strong>
<!--more-->

</strong>

<strong>MSNShell</strong> and <strong>SimpLite-Yahoo</strong> are two programs: <span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong>one encrypts MSN messages, and the other encrypts Yahoo instant messages</strong></span>. SimpLite also released an MSN-encrypting version, but because <strong>MSNShell</strong> has a Chinese interface, I'll use MSNShell for the MSN part. The principle behind MSNShell and SimpLite-Yahoo is not hard to understand: when you send an instant message, it's encrypted before it's sent to the other person. So even if a network administrator tries to record it, they'll only capture a bunch of gibberish. However, using MSNShell and <strong>SimpLite-Yahoo</strong> has one inconvenience: <span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong>not only do you (the sender) have to install the program, but the other person (the receiver) has to install it too</strong></span>. Otherwise, the messages you send them won't be encrypted.
<blockquote><strong>Note: Besides basic encryption, MSNShell has lots of other fun features. I'll cover them someday when I have time, but in the meantime, feel free to check out its </strong><a href="http://my.msnshell.com/"><strong>official website</strong></a><strong>. Also, be sure to note: if you want to encrypt messages, both computers must have the encryption program installed. For example, if computer A sends data to computer B, then both A and B must have the encryption program installed.</strong></blockquote>
